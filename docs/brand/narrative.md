# VertiFlow — brand narrative

Not a mission statement. This is the spine: who this is for, what it refuses to be, the
story as it can honestly be told, the tension it resolves, and the words it uses. Every
piece of copy — Instagram, site, journal — should trace back to this without restating it.

**The line: eight years of moving, made wearable.** VertiFlow is a clothing brand with a
real maker behind it. The story is his own practice — eight years of competing taught him
exactly what fails when you actually train in clothes, and that knowledge is the product.
PKBA is real and it matters, but it is evidence that Maxime builds things that last, not
the reason VertiFlow exists. See "Where PKBA appears, and how much" below for exactly how
that plays out page by page.

## Who this is for

Someone who has watched parkour clips and quietly concluded it isn't for them. Not because
they said so — because nothing they've seen suggested otherwise. Every video is the make,
never the miss. Every body is already elite. Every session looks like it started five years
before they'd be allowed to show up.

That person is the entire audience that matters. Not the athlete already training — they
don't need convincing, and they're a tiny market. Not the streetwear buyer who wants the
aesthetic without the sport — VertiFlow will happily sell them a hoodie, but it isn't built
for them. The brand is built for the person for whom "can I do it" is a real, unresolved
question, and the honest answer is yes, starting exactly where they are.

## What it refuses to be

- **A streetwear label that picked parkour as a backdrop.** The clothes exist because
  eight years of actually training came first — not because a club exists. If the founder's
  practice disappeared from the story, VertiFlow would be indistinguishable from a hundred
  generic drop-shipped athleisure accounts. It is not allowed to become that.
- **An intimidation reel.** Flawless lines, elite bodies, nothing but the make — that is
  what every other parkour account already posts, and it is precisely what keeps beginners
  out. VertiFlow's craft is allowed to look expensive; its subjects are not allowed to look
  unreachable. Rule of the whole brand: production value signals performance, subject
  matter holds the door open.
- **A documentary faked by a machine.** Real training photography is welcome and necessary.
  AI-generated imagery of a training session, a class, a crowd of practitioners, or anything
  that reads as "this happened at PKBA" is not — see `creative/house-look.md`. The brand can
  look this good in a campaign shot precisely because it never lies about what a session
  looks like.
- **A shortcut into PKBA's books.** Same founder, same community, different legal entity,
  different money. See the hard rule below — this one is non-negotiable, not a tone choice.
- **A revenue play wearing a community costume.** VertiFlow turns over roughly the cost of a
  few t-shirts a month. The number that actually matters is strangers who show up to train
  because of it. If a piece of copy optimises for sales at the expense of that, it's wrong,
  regardless of how well it converts.
- **A cult of the founder.** Maxime's credibility is used instrumentally, to make the door
  trustworthy. He is not the subject of the brand. The beginner is.

## The tension it resolves

Parkour looks like an elite sport because the only footage that travels is elite footage.
That's a media-selection bias, not a fact about the sport, but nobody explains that to a
stranger scrolling past a triple-flip reel. The result is a discipline that is, in reality,
radically enterable — no equipment, no team, no season — but that reads as closed.

**Production value signals performance, subject matter holds the door open.** Two halves,
one mechanism. If the craft is sloppy, nobody trusts that the person behind it is actually
good at the sport, and the "credibility to keep you once you're serious" half collapses. If
the subject is always the flawless line, nobody believes the sport is for them, and the
"door" half collapses. Both have to be true in the same frame: a shot as disciplined as a
sponsor would shoot, of someone who started last month, missing the jump, at 8pm on a
Tuesday. That single image is the whole positioning line made visible.

## The founder story, as it can honestly be told

Maxime competed in parkour for eight years at national level under the FFG (Fédération
Française de Gymnastique — parkour sits inside gymnastics' federal structure in France).
That's real, verifiable competitive history, not a claimed one. Eight years of actually
training in whatever he owned taught him what fails, long before it taught him anything
about running a brand: a fabric that tears on a landing, a cut that rides up mid-vault, a
seam that catches on a rail.

In November 2024 he started VertiFlow to fix that, piece by piece. It's a clothing brand
first — the founder's own training is where the product decisions come from, not a story
bolted on afterward.

Separately, in July 2025, he also founded PKBA — Parkour Bassin d'Arcachon — as a loi 1901
association, where he now serves as treasurer. It now has 80+ licenciés, and France 3
Nouvelle-Aquitaine has featured the club. PKBA is evidence that Maxime builds things that
last. It is not the reason VertiFlow exists, and copy should not treat it as one — see
"Where PKBA appears, and how much" below for exactly how much room it gets.

Then an injury paused all of it. The comeback took longer and was harder than starting had
ever been — harder than the eight years of competing, by Maxime's own account. That's not
a footnote to soften; it's the beat a nervous beginner identifies with most, because fear of
getting hurt is the single most common reason people never try parkour in the first place.
A founder who knows exactly what it costs to come back from a real setback, and did it
anyway, is a more credible door than a founder who never fell.

**What this story is not allowed to claim.** The old site's blog (`public/la-teste-de-buch-2025.html`,
`public/metz-2025.html`) covers French national parkour championship events — but as
journalism about the competition scene, naming other athletes (Sacha Fontaine, Islem Ben
Ahmed). Nothing in the repo documents Maxime's own competition results at those specific
events. Do not let a future draft infer personal podiums from those articles — that would
be exactly the kind of invented credibility this brand cannot afford, next to real
credentials that don't need embellishing. The instruction from the brief stands: eight
years FFG at national level, a club with 80+ licenciés, a France 3 feature. Real, and
sufficient. Nothing else is confirmed, so nothing else gets claimed.

Also not confirmed, as of this writing: the France 3 feature's air date, and PKBA's venue
address and size. Both reached this repo through a live web fetch, not through Maxime's own
sources, and he has not signed off on either. Say "France 3 Nouvelle-Aquitaine a fait un
reportage sur le club" — never attach a date to it. Say "le club a son propre local à
Gujan-Mestras" if a venue needs mentioning at all — never a street address or a m² figure,
until Maxime confirms them.

## Vocabulary

**Uses:**
- *tu*, always. Never *vous*.
- Short declarative sentences. A sentence that needs a comma to make its second point
  should usually be two sentences.
- *Commencer* — the word for a beginner's first move, used as the door's own name (`/commencer`).
- *Le club* for PKBA, *la marque* for VertiFlow, when a sentence needs to distinguish them
  without a full disclaimer.
- *L'essentiel* for the permanent nine-piece range — established already on the homepage
  and in the footer. Reuse it; don't invent a synonym.
- Concrete, sensory product language: what the fabric does during a movement, not what it's
  "crafted from." *"Tu sautes, tu réceptionnes, il suit"* is the house register. *"Conçu
  pour la performance"* is not.
- Named places: La Teste-de-Buch, Gujan-Mestras, Bordeaux, the Bassin d'Arcachon. Real
  geography reads as credible in a way "urban terrain" never will.
- Numbers that are exact and sourced: 8 ans, 80+ licenciés, neuf pièces. Not "des centaines
  de pratiquants" or other rounded-up vagueness. (Not 200 m² — that figure is unconfirmed,
  see above.)

**Never uses:**
- Superlatives doing the work a fact should do — *le meilleur*, *incroyable*, *unique en
  son genre*. If something is genuinely the best, say what makes it so and let the reader
  conclude it.
- *Communauté* as a filler noun standing in for a specific claim. Either name who's in it
  (des débutants, le club, les 80 licenciés) or don't reach for the word.
- Hype adjectives on product copy — *premium*, *exclusif*, *edition limitée* — none of
  which are true of a print-on-demand micro-entreprise range, and all of which read as
  imported marketing rather than something Maxime would actually say.
- Anything implying inventory, a team, or scale VertiFlow doesn't have. It's one person and
  a print-on-demand pipeline. Copy that accidentally implies otherwise (a royal "we" used
  carelessly, "notre équipe") undercuts the honesty that's the actual asset here.
- *VertiFlow x PKBA*, or any other co-branded lockup phrasing. This is covered in full
  below, but it belongs on this list too: it is a vocabulary error before it is a legal one.

## Where PKBA appears, and how much

VertiFlow is the brand. The product is the hero. PKBA is real, it's Maxime's, and it is not
why VertiFlow exists — the eight years of training are. Reaching for PKBA's numbers (80+
licenciés, a France 3 feature, a real venue) is tempting, because they're the most provable
credibility available. Leaning on them anyway turns VertiFlow's copy into a recruitment
page for a club it isn't. Keep to this, page by page:

- **`/a-propos`** — one mention, placed late in the page, framed explicitly as evidence
  Maxime builds real things — not as the reason VertiFlow exists. The founder's competitive
  practice is the spine of this page. The club is a footnote to it, not a chapter of it.
- **`/commencer`** — PKBA is legitimately central here. Questions 3 (`Where?`) and 4
  (`When?`) have no honest answer that doesn't name the club. Keep it — but the page's voice
  stays VertiFlow helping a beginner start, not a PKBA recruitment page. Don't let
  credentials (licenciés count, press coverage) creep into Question 1 (`What is this?`);
  save them for the questions they actually answer.
- **Home page, `/boutique`, product pages, `/journal`** — absent or incidental. The
  homepage's origin timeline lists VertiFlow's own milestones, not PKBA's. A journal post
  *about* PKBA (a clearly labelled announcement) is fine on its own terms; PKBA showing up
  inside unrelated product or shop copy is not.

If a page reaches for PKBA's credibility to make its point, that's a sign the point is
about PKBA, not VertiFlow — write it on `/commencer`, or leave it for PKBA's own site.

## The entity boundary — a hard rule, not a style note

VertiFlow is a micro-entreprise. PKBA is an association loi 1901 with open CERFA subsidy
dossiers at COBAS, Gujan-Mestras and La Teste for 2027, and Maxime is its treasurer. That
combination is precisely why this rule has teeth: subsidised association funds must never
appear, even ambiguously, to benefit a private commercial business. That's not a brand
preference, it's the condition under which the club's funding stays clean. A copywriter (or
model) treating this as a tone guideline rather than a compliance boundary will eventually
write the sentence that causes PKBA a real problem.

The rule: **same founder, same community, two separate entities.** Copy may say Maxime
built both. It must never read as though "VertiFlow" and "PKBA" are one organisation, one
budget, or one team.

This is not hypothetical — the repo already contains one instance of the mistake:
`public/images/pkba-partenariat.jpg`, a `VERTIFLOW ✕ PKBA` lockup graphic, is explicitly
flagged in `.superpowers/sdd/2026-08-22-vertiflow-storefront-monday/asset-decisions.md` as
unusable for exactly this reason. Treat that file as the canonical example of what not to
repeat, in image or in text.

**Safe phrasing:**
- *"VertiFlow est une micro-entreprise. Le club PKBA est une association loi 1901
  distincte."* — this is the footer's own language, verbatim (`src/components/site-footer.tsx`).
  Safe to extend with a line like *"Même fondateur, même terrain"* but keep the two-sentence
  structure that names each entity separately.
- *"J'ai fondé le club PKBA à côté de VertiFlow. Deux structures, un seul moi."*
- *"Beaucoup de gens qui portent VertiFlow s'entraînent au PKBA — un club indépendant, que
  j'ai fondé en plus de la marque."*
- *"Le club a son propre local à Gujan-Mestras."* (attributes the facility to the club,
  not to VertiFlow)

**Unsafe phrasing — do not write these:**
- *"VertiFlow x PKBA"* or any co-branded lockup, verbal or visual.
- *"Achète un t-shirt, soutiens le club."* / *"Chaque commande aide le PKBA."* — a direct
  causal claim tying commercial revenue to the association's activity. Even if casually
  true in spirit, this is the exact appearance the CERFA dossiers cannot risk. Never write
  it, even as a joke.
- *"Notre équipe VertiFlow-PKBA"* or *"on"* used to mean both entities interchangeably in
  the same sentence without disambiguating which one is acting.
- *"La boutique VertiFlow finance les séances gratuites."* — same problem as above, phrased
  as fact rather than pitch. Still not allowed.
- Any sentence where a reader could not, in one pass, tell you whether "the club" or "the
  brand" did the thing being described.

When in doubt: name the entity, not "we."
